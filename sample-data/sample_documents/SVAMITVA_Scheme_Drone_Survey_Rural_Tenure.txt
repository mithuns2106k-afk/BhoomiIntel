TITLE: SVAMITVA Scheme: Standard Operating Procedures for Drone-Based Cadastral Inhabited (Abadi) Land Survey and Property Card Issuance
AUTHOR: Ministry of Panchayati Raj & Survey of India
CATEGORY: Government Operational Guideline
THEME: Rural Land Tenure & Drone Mapping
DISTRICT_TAGS: Varanasi, Jodhpur, Pune
DATE: 2024-11-20

EXECUTIVE OVERVIEW:
SVAMITVA (Survey of Villages and Mapping with Improvised Technology in Village Areas) is a Central Sector Scheme providing formal documentation of property rights to rural household owners in inhabited (Abadi) areas using Continuously Operating Reference Stations (CORS) network and unmanned aerial vehicle (drone) technology.

1. METHODOLOGY OF CADASTRAL DRONE SURVEY:
Traditional rural inhabited abadi areas in India were historically left un-surveyed during colonial revenue settlements, leaving millions without legal titles (Sanad or Property Cards). Under SVAMITVA:
- First, the village boundaries and individual household parcels are marked with white limestone (chuna) by owners in the presence of Gram Panchayat officials.
- Survey of India deploys high-accuracy drone flights creating Orthorectified Imagery (ORI) with 5 cm ground sampling distance (GSD).
- Cadastral maps are extracted from imagery, verified on-ground via public inquiry (objection window of 30-45 days).

2. IMPACT ON DISPUTE PREVENTION AND RURAL CAPITAL FORMATION:
- In surveyed villages in Varanasi and Jodhpur districts, boundary demarcation disputes between neighbors dropped by 64% within 18 months post-issuance of SVAMITVA Property Cards.
- The property cards serve as official collateral, unlocking formal institutional credit and agricultural loan eligibility for rural homeowners.
- Gram Panchayats can generate local tax revenues based on verified GIS spatial property footprints.

3. CHALLENGES & DISPUTE RESOLUTION PROTOCOLS:
- Objections during the public verification phase primarily concern shared courtyards, pathway rights of way (Rasta disputes), and common village pond or grazing land encroachments.
- Standard guidelines mandate a three-tier grievance redressal committee at Panchayat, Tehsil, and District collectorate levels to settle boundary disputes before final digital card printing and blockchain registry hashing.
